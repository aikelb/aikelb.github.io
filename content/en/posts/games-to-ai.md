After 14 years making games, I took a job building AI agents.

It wasn't a dramatic pivot. Game development and AI work overlap more than you'd think — both involve managing complexity, designing systems that respond to user input, and dealing with unpredictable edge cases. The main difference is that in games you're building worlds for players. With AI agents you're building tools for other developers.

I still write Python every day. I still think about user experience, just in a different context. The stakes feel lower in a good way — nobody's leaving a Steam review if the agent hallucinates a function name.

The hardest part was admitting I was ready for something new. I'd built my identity around being a game developer. Letting that go, even partially, felt like a loss. But I still tinker with Unity on weekends and keep up with what friends are shipping. The skills transfer. The community doesn't disappear. You just add a new chapter.

If you're considering a similar move: the technical transition is manageable. The identity shift is the real work.

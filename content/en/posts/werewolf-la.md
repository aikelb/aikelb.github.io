In early 2024 we shipped [An American Werewolf in L.A.](https://playmedusa.itch.io/werewolf) on Nintendo Switch. No publisher, no marketing budget, just a small team and a lot of late nights.

The Switch port was humbling. We had been building for PC and mobile for years, but console certification is a different beast. Memory budgets, platform-specific save systems, controller rebinding screens you never think about until Nintendo asks for them. Every screen had to work in both handheld and docked modes. Every menu had to respond to a D-pad, not just touch.

The game itself is an open-world driving sandbox where you play as a werewolf causing chaos in a parody of Los Angeles. It launched on Steam and Meta Quest first, then we spent about six months reworking it for Switch. The hardware isn't powerful, so we had to be careful with draw calls, texture atlasing, and keeping the frame rate stable while the player tears through traffic at high speed.

I'm not going to pretend it was a huge commercial success, and it wasn't. But seeing our game on the Nintendo eShop, next to titles from studios with actual budgets, felt like crossing a finish line we'd been running toward for years.

If you're attempting a console port for the first time: budget more time than you think for certification. The technical work is the easy part. The paperwork and compliance checks are what get you.

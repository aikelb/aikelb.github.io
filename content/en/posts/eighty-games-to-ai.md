Between PlayMedusa and Aurita Games we've shipped more than 80 titles: mobile games, client work, prototypes that grew into products. Every one of them built with the best resources we had at the time.

Almost everything I've done in my career happened under PlayMedusa, from a Barbie game to mentoring at game jams to the Exel accelerator in Riyadh. Working alongside Luis all those years shaped how I work, how I teach, and how I see a lot of things in life. I'm grateful for it.

A few of them I hold especially close. *Oddy Smog's Misadventure* is the reason I joined PlayMedusa in the first place. *Slider* started as my final degree project and grew into a real mobile game. And *An American Werewolf in L.A.* reaching the Nintendo Switch was a milestone for us as a team. Publishing a game on a Nintendo console was something we'd been working toward for years.

After fourteen years of making games, I took a job building AI.

The real shift wasn't the work. It was trading the freelancer life for an employee's. Both have their pros and cons, but I got used to the new one faster than I expected. Working from home with a great team has a lot to do with that.

It's changed how I work, too: I barely write code these days. Mostly I guide an AI to build what I have in mind, because it gets there faster than I would on my own. Some days that still feels strange to say out loud. I don't really know where these tools are heading, but I suspect we'll find out sooner than later.

Between PlayMedusa and Aurita Games I've shipped over 80 titles. That number sounds impressive on a CV but honest? Most of them were small. Mobile hyper-casual games, client work, prototypes that turned into products. A few stood out.

What I learned across that volume isn't what you'd expect. It's not about engines or patterns — those change every few years anyway. It's about finishing things. Shipping a mediocre game teaches you more than planning a great one. It's about knowing when to cut a feature instead of polishing it for another month. It's about reading a client's email and understanding what they actually need, not what they asked for.

The games I'm most proud of aren't the biggest ones. They're the ones where the team was small, the scope was realistic, and we actually shipped on time. *An American Werewolf in L.A.* made it to Switch. A Barbie mobile game we built at Relativity crossed 10 million downloads — and honestly, that one felt mostly like being in the right place at the right time.

If you're early in your career and worried about not having a hit: don't be. Ship things. Finish them. The volume compounds in ways you don't notice until you look back.

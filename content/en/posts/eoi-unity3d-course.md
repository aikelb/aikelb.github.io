In 2022 we ran a 110-hour Unity3D course at **EOI** (Escuela de Organización Industrial), a Spanish business school. Luis did most of the design on the PlayMedusa side; my part was the teaching, and I covered the areas I know best.

That meant the patterns I lean on most in game code (observer, component, state machines), game feel and juice, a bit of rendering and performance, and the unglamorous work of actually building and shipping a project.

It was a postgraduate course, and most students arrived from regular software engineering. A good part of the early work was loosening the habits that serve you well in business software but get in the way of real-time, interactive code.

110 hours sounds like a lot until you start. Finishing a working prototype was a requirement to pass, and by the end every student had one.
